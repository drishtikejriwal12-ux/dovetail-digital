Fieldnote

A visual identity for an independent notebook maker, built around the quiet confidence of well-kept paper.

Type
Brand identity
Timeline
6 weeks (concept)
Role
Strategy, identity, guidelines
Shipped
Logo system, colour and type, covers, packaging

This is a fictional concept project. There is no real client, and nothing here describes real results.

The challenge

Independent notebook makers compete with large, glossy stationery brands. Fieldnote needed to feel unmistakably handmade without looking rustic, and to work as well on a shelf as on a screen.

The approach

We treated the brand as the page it sells: restrained, warm and made to be written on. One earthy colour does the talking, an italic voice appears only when the brand wants to be personal, and everything else steps back.

The process

Four steps, one thread.

  1. 01

    Discover

    We looked at how paper goods are bought and kept: at the shelf, as gifts, and years later in a drawer. We collected what makes a notebook feel considered and noted what was shared across the brands we admired.

    Output: audit and a one-page positioning statement

  2. 02

    Define

    We settled on three brand words (quiet, tactile, exact) and tested every early idea against them. Ideas that shouted were cut.

    Output: brand platform and voice notes

  3. 03

    Design

    Wordmark first, in black and white, before any colour was added. Then the palette, built from paper, graphite and a single clay red, and a type pairing of an italic serif and a plain sans.

    Output: logo system, colour and type

  4. 04

    Deliver

    We applied the system to covers, labels and a belly band, then wrote guidelines short enough to be read, with do and don't pairs for each rule.

    Output: guidelines and applied mockups

What shipped

Identity in use

The pieces that make up the shipped identity.

What we took from it

Constraint made it better

Limiting the palette to one accent colour made every other decision easier and the range more recognisable.

Test in black first

A mark that holds in one colour will survive every material it is printed on.

Guidelines are a product

Short, visual rules got used. Long ones would not have.
